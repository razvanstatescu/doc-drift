When to use this runbook

The settlement workers normally scale themselves: a HorizontalPodAutoscaler keeps them between workers.min and workers.max on CPU. The autoscaler does not see queue depth yet (that is {{PAY:autoscale-workers-queue-depth}}), so scale by hand when:

  • The Pending settlement jobs panel stays above 5,000 for 10 minutes.

  • You expect a burst, such as a merchant batch import or the first hour after a PSP outage.

Limits

The chart sets the bounds. Stay inside them; going above the maximum needs a database review, because every worker pod opens its own connection pool.

Steps

  1. Check the current count and what the autoscaler wants:

    bash

  2. Pick a count. Rule of thumb: 1 worker per 1,500 pending jobs, at least 2, at most 8.

  3. Scale from the root of the payments-api repo. The script refuses counts outside the chart limits and waits for the rollout.

    bash

  4. Watch the pending jobs panel. It should fall within 15 minutes.

  5. When the queue is under 500, let the autoscaler take over again. It will settle back toward workers.min within about 5 minutes; you do not need to scale down by hand.

If the queue does not move

  • Workers are up but idle: check that settlement enqueue is not paused. See for the resume step.

  • Workers are failing: look for settlement batch failed in the worker logs. A PSP outage looks like this; follow .

  • Database connection errors: you are near the connection limit. Scale down to the previous count and page the database on-call.
