## 0. Why v5

Razvan (2026-11-17): v4 is a bit long and boring for a product that is not complex; a friend who mostly uses Jira did
not understand what it does. Make the problem and the solution very clear, with what is on screen matching what is
said, and give it more editing energy.

What the tests found (R4 and the viewers): no sentence ever says plainly what it does and for whom; about 40 terms go
unexplained (Rovo, MCP, anchor, PR, space...); the voice is silent 30% of the time while the screen shows 5 to 20 words
a second; the story is told twice; key words miss their picture (the tooltip names `values.yaml` under "the exact
change", the old command shows in amber under "it doesn't repeat the old step", a rename illustrates "anything
bigger"); the real footage is one-second thumbnails; the opening collage is unreadable; the end does not say what it is
or where to get it.

## 1. Decisions (D41)

1. **About 100 s, one story told forward.** The 3 a.m. problem, then what Doc Drift is and for whom (voiced and on
   screen by 0:27), then one continuous real loop (link, merge, flagged in 19 s, the exact change, no AI), agents told,
   a person fixes it, Jira, the end with the link. No opening collage, no second telling, no stale-again callback.
2. **Plain words, explained at first use:** Confluence ("Atlassian's wiki"), Rovo ("Atlassian's AI"), Drift Reviewer
   ("our agent"), MCP ("an open standard for AI tools"), Possibly stale ("it may be out of date"). Never voiced or
   shown as a must-read: PR as an abbreviation, byline, anchor, deterministic, matcher, score, citePolicy, space, PLAT.
3. **Picture equals words.** At every moment the focal element is the thing the voice names. Every word set on screen
   is a word the voice says (plus proof tags, the measured-time tag, the sample-data label and the URL).
4. **Real footage is the proof, shown big.** One continuous real block for the core loop (S08 to S12), and real
   captures at reading size elsewhere; crafted visuals for the problem, the one diagram (S07), and the end. Real
   footage is made gripping by the camera, not shrunk into thumbnails (R6).
5. **The close** says what it works with, where to get it, and the tested line "Docs that know when the code moved."
   ("Catch the slip before your agents do." is out: two of four test viewers could not parse it.)

## 4. Rules for the picture

v4's look and rules stay (V4.md section 2: the Day world, one amber = drift, read square and move in 3D, the frame never
freezes, proofs at most 1 film px per capture px, the honesty list). v5 adds:

**Rhythm (R6 section 6; QA measures it with `out/v5/r6/scripts/analyze6.py`).** v4 was not under-cut, it was uniformly
busy: 20.7 moving transitions a minute and a 1.5 s median shot (Pot 9.5 and 2.7 s, Satchel 7.4 and 4.3 s).
- Median shot in the body 3 to 4.5 s; at least 3 shots of 8 s or more (one is the real merge-to-flag take); 70% or more
  of body time in shots of 4 s or more. Shots under 1 s only in bursts at section changes.
- 8 to 10 moving transitions a minute; big transitions (whip, zoom-through, slip cut) 2 or 3 times each in the whole
  film, at section changes. Inside a section: carry cuts (one object crosses the cut) or a plain cut on an action.
- Something new every 1.2 to 1.5 s, but 60% or more of it inside a stable composition (a mark, a word landing, a swap,
  a click, a number rolling, a spotlight moving), and at most 15 to 20 whole-frame changes a minute.
- A held shot stays alive: camera creep, one visible process (a clock, a stream, a travelling cursor), a small event
  every 1 to 1.5 s, and the score moving on across a bar line. Never two holds in a row.

**Picture equals words.**
- At every moment the focal element is the thing the voice is naming.
- Every word set on screen is a word the voice says. The exceptions are proof tags, the measured-time tag, the
  sample-data label, a small context label, and the URL.
- One statement at a time, 2 to 5 words. Two voices of type:
  - functional: a 12 to 14 frame mask reveal, for labels, code and results;
  - expressive: KineticLine, or type that becomes UI, at most 4 times in the film.

**Real footage, directed at full size (R6 section 6.5).**
- One continuous real take of 8 s or more at full bleed: the merge to the flag.
- Real captures elsewhere at reading size, never as one-second thumbnails.
- Zoom to the detail the voice names, at most 1 film px per capture px. Our 2x captures put 14 px UI text at 28 px,
  so crop to about half the browser width for a full-frame detail.
- A spotlight veils, softens and drains the rest of the frame.
- Ramp every wait at 4 to 10x, with a speed label and a clock in real seconds; play at 1x for 0.3 s around every
  click and result.
- Real clicks land on beats. The kit's cursor appears only at recorded click points.
- The camera moves at most every 2 to 3 s inside a take. Tag each take once. Masks from `captures.json` are always
  drawn.

**Never show:**
- "Doc Drift Sentinel" (the `p4-comment` author, the notification in `p2-before-reload` and `p2-flag-rec`, the relay
  settings title, any v4 lockup);
- "Dana" or any Owner, Deciders or Severity table row (the runbook's and ADR-015's header tables: crop above them; R6's
  merge prototype's last frame shows "Dana (Platform on-call lead)" and must be recropped);
- "(Development)";
- "Unanchored";
- the byline tooltip naming `values.yaml` anywhere near "the exact change";
- the old command anywhere in frame while Drift Reviewer answers;
- "bigger" over a rename;
- matcher, score, threshold, `citePolicy`, or more than three lines of JSON;
- a generic or plain-Rovo assistant giving the caveat;
- "No AI" after S16;
- GitHub, implied in any way.

**Sound (R6 section 6.7).**
- A hit's transient lands on the contact frame or 1 frame after.
- 60% or more of hard and carry cuts sit on a strong accent.
- Every section change is a J-cut: the next section's first sound starts 4 to 10 frames before its picture.
- 25 to 35 designed effects a minute: `slip` 6 times or fewer, `snap` once, `merge` once.
- One silence, the half bar before the flag.

## 5. Picture, line by line

R5 section 5's "What you see" column is the base, with the techniques below (numbers refer to R6 section 4). The
prototypes in `src/v5/lab/` (`DirectedRecording`, `UnderThePage`, `EditType`, `CarryCuts`) show four of them working;
promote them into the kit rather than copying.

**PROBLEM (S01 to S05), Night, medium pace, 5 or 6 shots.** v4's A1 parts (clock roll, alert card, Night runbook
`PageTwin`, shell error, change card, generic assistant), held longer.
- S01: `03:07` rolls in and the alert card lifts.
- S02: the runbook page, with a small label "A page in Confluence". The Mist band marks step 2's
  `./scripts/rollback.sh` on "rollback script".

  `scripts/rollback.sh → scripts/rollback-v2.sh · 6 days ago` (the one amber). The dead path is the object carried
  through this section.
- S04: back on the page's "Reviewed 34 days ago ✓" (Mist band on "review date"). The change card hangs outside the
  page, and the statement "A review date can't see a code change." sets beside it.
- S05: the generic "AI assistant" streams step 2's old command, with a source chip "Payments rollback runbook"; the
  command is underlined on "old step".

**PROMISE (S06 to S07): the turn, a burst then one long hold.**
- S06:
  - a carry cut from Night to Day on the dead path (CarryCuts), then the Day slip cut;
  - the mark lands, its middle band slips amber, and the lockup reads "Doc Drift";
  - on "for teams with docs in Confluence" one line sets under it, and on "Atlassian's wiki" a small gloss follows

**LOOP (S08 to S12): the real block, about 22 s, long takes.**

- S12: a real click opens the review panel (`p4-review-rec` 25.5 to 28.5 s, or the still `p4-panel`). Spotlight and
